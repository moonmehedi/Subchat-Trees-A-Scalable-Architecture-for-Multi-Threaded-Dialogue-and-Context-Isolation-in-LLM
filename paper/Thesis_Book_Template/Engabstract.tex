%\section*{Predicting Green Space of a Location Through Historical Image Analysis And Predictive Modeling}
%\vspace{10mm}
  
\justifying
         In the race of urbanization, the cities have lost their greenery. Cities are becoming densely populated with time and green spaces are shrinking fast. There is a growing need for various services, such as housing, workplaces and other facilities such as schools and hospitals, to meet the needs of the local population. The forests are also impacted; the damage is not limited to metropolitan areas. Forest trees are being chopped down for a variety of reasons, including the production of wood and other building materials, fuel, land development, and plenty more. Urban planners put a lot of effort into figuring out how to use every acre of land productively when designing urban landscapes. Environmental factors are therefore disregarded. However, the negligence has reached a point where there is no choice but to restore the damage and integrate green space into communities. Hence, the objective of this research is to propose a predictive system which can give a predictive value of the green view index of an area based on its history of images of the past years at 5-year intervals. To achieve this objective, firstly, traditional deep learning and ensemble Learning techniques were implemented with a publicly available and self-made data sets containing 5108 and 1705 images respectively. All the models were evaluated using suitable performance metrics and the ensemble learning approach was found to perform better \textbf{(with accuracy, precision, and dice-coefficient of 0.94, 0.96 and 0.91)}. Among the four RNN models trained, LSTM achieved the lowest error metrics \textbf{(MAE of 0.2707, MSE of 0.1492 and an RMSE of 0.3862)}. Finally, a two-module framework is proposed to forecast the change in a location's future green view index consisting of individual Siamese Models and an LSTM Model. By leveraging satellite imagery and time series data for green space detection and prediction, a contribution can be made to offer insights into urban planning and environmental
conservation.
